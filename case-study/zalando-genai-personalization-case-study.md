## 3. Industry & Business Context

Zalando is one of Europe's largest fashion and lifestyle e-commerce platforms, competing in a market where online retailers are under growing pressure to move beyond pure search-and-buy transactions toward AI-driven, inspiration-led discovery that keeps exploratory shoppers engaged.

## 4. Problem Statement

Zalando was building GenAI personalization into two customer touchpoints: a personalized search leaderboard for exploratory shoppers, whom its intent-driven search under-served, and personalized product lists and boards, which were not tied to each customer's recent actions, preferences, or style interests. However, delivery of both workstreams was held back by several issues:

- **Ineffective project management**: the project was not managed effectively.
- **No expertise in the client team**: the client team had no expert to guide delivery.
- **Release delays**: releases slipped behind schedule.
- **Global–local communication and coordination gaps**: the local and global teams struggled to communicate and coordinate.
- **No Scrum practices or standardized workflows**: Scrum practices were not in place, and the team had no standardized workflows.

## 5. Solution Delivered

*Scope note: the solution was to onboard a dedicated Project Coordinator to manage both workstreams end to end — not to build the GenAI features themselves (the "Your Weekly Trend Leaderboard" on search, and "GenAI Product List" with LLM-personalized lists), which were engineered by the EU and China-Shenzhen dev teams and launched as phased experiments across selected markets.*

| Feature / Capability | Description |
|---|---|
| Dedicated project management | A single owner for planning, tracking, and steering both workstreams, replacing the ineffective project management that had let delivery drift. |
| Scrum practices & standardized workflows | Introduction of Scrum ceremonies and standardized delivery workflows shared across both workstreams and both locations. |
| Release planning & schedule control | Roadmap-to-backlog planning, risk and dependency tracking, and prioritization to bring releases back on schedule. |
| Global–local coordination | A bridge between the EU and China-Shenzhen teams across languages and working styles, with regular status reporting to local and global management. |
| Delivery expertise for the client team | Oversight of official project documentation readiness, plus UAT leadership, filling the delivery expertise gap in the client team. |

## 6. My Role & Responsibilities

**Title:** Project Coordinator (China-EU)

I coordinated and managed two parallel GenAI personalization workstreams — personalized search leaderboard and personalized product list — bridging the EU and China-Shenzhen teams co-building them across different languages, working styles, and cooperation norms.

- **Cross-location delivery coordination** — running agile ceremonies and tracking risks and dependencies across both workstreams and across China-Shenzhen and EU stakeholders (Principal Engineer, Experiment Analysts, Portfolio Manager, PMO).
- **Schedule tracking** — monitoring project activities across both workstreams against the plan, and following up early so they stayed on track.
- **Project documentation oversight** — reviewing the required official project documents (BRDs, technical designs, experiment designs) to ensure they were up to date and overseen by their owners, and ready for review, implementation, or sharing at the agreed timepoints.
- **Roadmap-to-backlog planning** — partnering with the Product Manager to turn the GenAI roadmap into delivery plans, and managing a prioritized backlog across experiments, feature rollouts, and tech debt.
- **UAT leadership** — using Databricks to analyze test results, defect patterns, and output-quality trends, and prioritizing fixes for sign-off.
- **Reporting and ad hoc support** — supporting the Product Manager, Tech Lead, and dev team, and running regular progress reporting to local and global management.
