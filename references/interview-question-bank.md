# Interview Question Bank

Questions for every interview aspect except **candidate assessment focuses**, which `assess-candidate` generates per candidate. How many questions each aspect gets is set by the allocation in `assess-candidate` Step 6.

| Aspect | Section | When to use |
|---|---|---|
| Analysis focus | [Business](#business-analysis) · [System](#system-analysis) · [Data](#data-analysis) | Only the 1–2 focuses confirmed for the role |
| Plus responsibility | [Project Manager](#project-manager) · [Product Owner](#product-owner) · [Scrum Master](#scrum-master) · [Quality Assurance](#quality-assurance) | Only the plus responsibility confirmed for the role, if any |
| Agile methodology | [Agile](#agile-methodology) | Every interview |
| AI use in daily work | [AI](#ai-use-in-daily-work) | Every interview |
| Soft skills | [Soft skills](#soft-skills) | Every interview |

**Type:** **Mandatory** questions are always selected when their section is used and count toward its allocation; **Optional** questions are selected per candidate, with a rationale. **Competency** names match `references/seniority-competency-definition-business-analysis.md` where one applies. **Strong answer includes:** points the interviewer listens for — not read out to the candidate. Where questions in different sections share a theme, **Perspective** marks the angle a strong answer takes in that section.

**When editing the bank:** a section's Mandatory count must not exceed its smallest allocation in `assess-candidate` Step 6, and should stay below it so Optional picks can tailor the interview.

| Section | Max Mandatory | Smallest allocation occurs when |
|---|---|---|
| Business | 10 | Primary focus, with a secondary focus and a plus responsibility |
| System · Data | 4 | Secondary focus, with a plus responsibility |
| Each plus responsibility | 3 | Always |
| Agile | 3 | Always |
| Soft skills | 2 | Always |
| AI | 2 | Always |

## Business Analysis

| ID | Type | Competency | Question | Strong answer includes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| BA-01 | Mandatory | Business analysis activities planning | How would you define the role of a BA on a delivery project? What are the core responsibilities, and where do they end and other roles (PM, PO, QA, developers) begin? | BA owns understanding the problem and the quality of requirements; PM owns delivery; PO owns value and priority; QA verifies; developers design the solution; clear handoffs and known overlaps |
| BA-02 | Mandatory | BA SDLC awareness | Walk me through one project from intake to production. What did you personally own at each stage, and what did you hand over to others? | Clear stage-by-stage account; personal ownership distinguished from the team's; artifacts produced; handoffs and sign-offs; lessons learned |
| BA-03 | Optional | Business Analysis Approach Definition | You join a new project as the only BA. There's no agreed BA process, the client expects the first stories in three weeks, and the client prefers formal documents while the developers want a lean backlog. How would you define your BA approach and get both sides to agree to it? | Assesses context first; tailors ways of working, deliverables, and tools to each audience; balances documents with backlog; agrees the approach explicitly; revisits it as the project evolves |
| BA-04 | Optional | Efforts estimation | A new feature needs discovery workshops, a process model, about a dozen user stories, and a review of test cases, while you also support another team part-time. The project manager asks when the first stories will be ready. How would you estimate your work and plan your time? | Breaks work into tasks; named technique (e.g. three-point, T-shirt sizing); ranges and assumptions for unknowns; capacity for the other team; sequences work to deliver first stories early; re-estimates as things become clearer |
| BA-05 | Optional | Understanding the organization | You join a client in an industry you've never worked in. Your first stakeholder workshop is in two weeks, the client uses a lot of internal jargon, and the existing documentation is mostly technical. How would you learn the domain and the organisation quickly enough to run that workshop credibly? | Glossary of jargon; org structure and key stakeholders; business goals; how work actually flows (shadowing, walkthroughs); domain research; checks understanding with a domain expert |
| BA-06 | Optional | Elicitation of information | You have a 90-minute discovery workshop with eight stakeholders from different departments for a new initiative. They have only a vague idea of what they want, and the sponsor expects clear requirements afterwards. How would you prepare, run, and follow up the session so it produces requirements the stakeholders have confirmed? | Objectives and agenda shared in advance; pre-interviews; facilitation techniques; parking lot; plays back what was heard; written summary with owners for open questions; confirmation from stakeholders |
| BA-07 | Optional | Elicitation Techniques | You need requirements from three sources: field staff who are hard to schedule, senior managers who each have strong and conflicting opinions, and a legacy system no one fully understands. Which elicitation technique would you use for each, and why? | Field staff: observation, short interviews, or surveys; managers: individual interviews then a facilitated workshop; legacy system: document and interface analysis, reverse engineering; justifies each choice |
| BA-08 | Optional | Business need definition | A stakeholder requests a new feature: users should be able to bulk-edit records from a list "like in a spreadsheet". They describe the screen in detail, but not the problem it solves or who it's for. Before writing any stories, how would you uncover the business need behind the request, define the feature's goal and success measures, and confirm the requested solution is the right one? | Asks "why" to reach the root problem; identifies users; defines goal and measurable success criteria; explores alternative solutions; confirms with the stakeholder before writing stories |
| BA-09 | Optional | BA artifacts documentation | You're asked to improve an existing business process. How would you model the as-is process, and how would you use that model to identify what needs to improve and why? | Notation such as BPMN or swimlanes; suitable level of detail; roles, handoffs, inputs and outputs, timings, pain points; validates with participants; analyses bottlenecks, waste, and rework |
| BA-10 | Optional | Solution definition | A requirement says users must be notified when a record they own changes status. The development team proposes three options: real-time notifications (most effort, needs a new service), an hourly email digest (quick to build, but delayed), or an in-app indicator shown at next login (cheapest, but easy to miss). How would you assess the options and reach and document a decision? | Clarifies how quickly users really need to know; compares options against requirements, non-functional needs, cost, effort, and risk; involves business and tech lead; records the decision and rationale |
| BA-11 | Mandatory | BA artifacts documentation | What's wrong with this user story, and how would you fix it? *"As an operations user, I want to bulk-approve pending orders from the order list, and the system should automatically email the customer and update the finance system in real time, so that I can work faster. AC: (1) User can select multiple orders and click Approve. (2) Approved orders are sent to the finance system immediately. (3) The customer receives a confirmation email. (4) Approval must be fast, even for large volumes. (5) Only valid orders can be approved."* | Vague role (who may approve; permissions); weak value statement; bulk approval, notification, and finance integration bundled — split them; solution prescribed ("email", "real time") without the need; AC 4 untestable and a non-functional need — measurable NFR; AC 5 "valid" undefined — needs rules; missing partial-success and failure handling (invalid orders, finance system rejects or times out), selection limit, duplicate approval, audit trail; rewrites as measurable Given/When/Then AC including failure paths |
| BA-12 | Mandatory | Non-functional requirements | Stakeholders say the new system must be "fast, always available, and secure" but give no figures. What kind of requirements are these, and how would you gather what you need so the team can build and test against them? | Identifies them as non-functional requirements (performance, availability, security), distinct from functional requirements; elicits by asking about business impact (user numbers, peak volumes, working hours and time zones, cost of downtime, data sensitivity, regulations); uses baselines from the current system and benchmarks; involves architects, operations, and security; measurable targets with conditions (e.g. response time at peak load, uptime); agreed with business and tech; testable |
| BA-13 | Optional | Business analysis information management | After weeks of discovery you have meeting notes, emails, workshop outputs, and stakeholder documents scattered across tools. How would you turn this raw information into a structured, reusable knowledge base, and how would you keep it maintained as the project evolves? | Single source of truth; structure by topic or feature; templates and naming conventions; tags and links; version history; clear ownership and regular review |
| BA-14 | Optional | Business analysis information management | Requirements change several times a week. Business stakeholders only hear about changes in meetings, developers are building from an outdated version, and QA can't tell which version their tests cover. How would you make sure each group can quickly find the latest information and learns promptly when something relevant to them changes? | Single source with version history; change log; notifications tailored to each group; clear requirement status; links to tests; changes reviewed in team ceremonies |
| BA-15 | Optional | Requirements traceability management | After a release, a stakeholder reports that one of their original requests was never delivered. You find it was discussed in a workshop but never became a story, while another story was built but only half its acceptance criteria were tested. How would you set up traceability from business request to requirement and acceptance criteria, through implementation and testing, to delivery, so that nothing is missed? | Links from request to requirement, AC, code, tests, and release; linked work items or a traceability matrix; coverage checks at each stage; gap review before release |
| BA-16 | Optional | BA Quality control | Sprint planning is in two days, and QA wants to start writing test cases from one of your stories. It still has a draft acceptance criterion, an unanswered question to the business owner, and a dependency on another team's component that isn't confirmed. How do you decide whether it's ready for development and test design, what do you check, and who do you involve? | Checks AC complete and testable; open questions resolved; dependencies confirmed; applies a Definition of Ready; involves business owner, PO, developers, and QA; pushes back or splits if not ready |
| BA-17 | Optional | BA Quality control | Three departments have submitted requirements for the same new system. Two define the same status differently, one wants an approval step that another wants removed, and all three marked every item as must-have. How would you clarify the business goal behind each requirement, get a clear decision from the right stakeholders, and make sure that decision sticks? | Asks why for each requirement; shared glossary for definitions; identifies the decision maker; prioritisation technique; facilitated session; documented decision and sign-off |
| BA-18 | Optional | BA Risk management | Over the last two sprints, several of your stories caused rework: one's acceptance criteria missed an edge case that became a production defect, one was read differently by the developer and the tester, and one was built as written but didn't match what the business expected. How would you find the root causes, and what would you change in how you write and review requirements to catch these risks before development starts? | Root cause for each case; joint refinement with developers and testers; edge-case checklist; examples or Given/When/Then AC; business walkthrough before development; tracks rework trends |
| BA-19 | Mandatory | Requirements approval and prioritization management | The product owner has ranked the backlog by business value. While refining it, you find the top item depends on another team's unfinished system, and a low-ranked item is a prerequisite for several others. How would you present these dependencies so the product owner can make an informed decision, and what delivery order would you propose? | Maps technical, data, and team dependencies; explains impact on sequence and risk; presents options and trade-offs; PO keeps the decision; proposes an order that unblocks value early |
| BA-20 | Optional | Requirements and Change management | Two weeks before release, a senior stakeholder asks to add "just one field" with a new validation rule. You find it also affects an integration with another system, two reports, and test cases that have already passed. How would you assess and communicate the impact, what options would you put forward, and how would you take the request through change control? | **Perspective:** the change-control process and impact analysis (SS-04 covers the candidate's own handling of pushback). Impact across integration, reports, and tests; effort and risk; options (defer, reduce, accept and move the date); formal change request; decision by the right authority |
| BA-21 | Optional | Requirements and Change management | A new regulation takes effect in six months. It limits how long personal data can be kept, requires explicit consent before data is shared with third parties, and requires an audit trail for approvals. Your product's requirements were written before it existed. How would you find which processes, requirements, and systems are affected, update the documentation, and show the business the product will be compliant? | **Perspective:** impact of a regulatory change on processes and requirements (SA-13, DA-10, and AI-02 cover personal data from other angles). Breaks regulation into requirements; traces to processes and systems; gap analysis; involves compliance; traceability as evidence |
| BA-22 | Optional | BA Stakeholders management | Mid-project, you learn that a third-party service your main feature depends on will be retired before your launch date, and no replacement has been agreed. The sponsor expects an on-time release next month. How would you communicate this risk, and what mitigation options would you prepare? | **Perspective:** impact on requirements and scope (PM-05 covers risk tracking and the plan). Which features depend on the service; alternative solutions; tells the right people early — to whom, how soon, with facts and options |
| BA-23 | Optional | BA Stakeholders management | You join a project where the sponsor rarely attends meetings, the operations team who will use the system daily hasn't been consulted, a compliance officer can block the release, and the development team works in another time zone. How would you analyse these stakeholders, and how would you adapt your communication with each? | Stakeholder mapping (e.g. power/interest); tailors what, how, and how often to communicate with each; engages operations and compliance early; asynchronous updates across time zones |

## System Analysis

| ID | Type | Competency | Question | Strong answer includes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| SA-01 | Mandatory | System interactions | A new system must work with four others: two internal systems, an external partner, and a legacy system. How would you document its interactions with these systems, and which tools or techniques would you use? | Context diagram; sequence diagrams for key scenarios; interface catalogue (data exchanged, direction, frequency, owner); validates with each system team |
| SA-02 | Optional | System analysis | You're asked to analyse a legacy system before it's replaced. Its documentation is ten years old, the original developers have left, and users say some screens are never used while others are business-critical. How would you work out what the system actually does and where it could be improved? | Interviews users and support; observes usage; analyses logs and data; reviews code or database with developers; documents functions, business rules, data, and integrations; prioritises by business criticality |
| SA-03 | Optional | Architecture awareness | Your organisation is splitting one large application into separate services, each owned by a different team. How does this change the way you write and organise requirements, especially for features that span several services? | Requirements per service and team; cross-service features decomposed with clear contracts; end-to-end flows documented; coordination across owners |
| SA-04 | Mandatory | API requirements | Your team is building a new API for an internal web front end and two external partners. What would you include in its requirements, and how would you make them clear enough for developers and partners to build against? | Operations; request and response structures; validation rules; errors; authentication; versioning; non-functional needs; examples; a standard format such as OpenAPI |
| SA-05 | Optional | API integration | The business wants to use a third-party service through its API instead of building the capability in-house. Comparing the vendor's API documentation with your requirements, you find two gaps: the API doesn't provide some data your users need, and it can't update a record once it's created, which one of your business rules requires. How would you check whether the API meets the business and technical requirements, and what would you do about the gaps? | Fit-gap analysis; asks the vendor about roadmap or extensions; workarounds; business impact of each gap; options (adapt process, customise, alternative vendor); decision with stakeholders |
| SA-06 | Optional | Exception handling | A new feature sends information to another system, which sometimes rejects it or doesn't respond. Developers ask what should happen in each case. How would you work out and specify these requirements? | Identifies each failure scenario; what the user sees; retries; what gets logged; who is alerted; manual recovery; agreed with business and tech; written as requirements and AC |
| SA-07 | Optional | Integration patterns | When a user submits a request, they expect to know straight away whether it's accepted, which depends on an availability check in another system. Once accepted, three other teams' systems must be updated: one by the end of the day, one within minutes, and one that is often offline for maintenance. How would you analyse the business needs behind each step to decide which interactions must happen immediately (synchronous) and which can happen later (asynchronous)? | Availability check synchronous because the user needs an immediate answer; downstream updates asynchronous; queueing and retries for the offline system; failure handling and data consistency |
| SA-08 | Optional | Interface specification | A partner organisation will send your system a file every night to load new records, and nothing has been agreed with them yet. What would you need to agree and document with the partner so both sides can build and test against the same specification? | File format and field definitions; schedule and delivery method; validation rules; handling of late, duplicate, or partly invalid files; acknowledgements; versioning; test data; formal sign-off |
| SA-09 | Optional | Data modelling | A new feature needs to store several new types of information, some linked to existing records through one-to-many and many-to-many relationships. How would you capture the data model, and how would you validate it with the development team before build starts? | Entities, attributes, relationships, and cardinality; ER diagram; keys and constraints; walkthrough with developers; checks against use cases and sample data |
| SA-10 | Optional | Data flow analysis | A new process passes data through three systems, each with its own field names, formats, and code values. How would you document the data flow and field mappings so the teams owning each system build consistent transformations? | Data flow diagram; source-to-target mapping; format and code-value translations; transformation rules; ownership; reviewed with each team |
| SA-11 | Optional | System behaviour modelling | A record moves through draft, submitted, approved, rejected, cancelled, and closed statuses. Some transitions are allowed only for certain roles, and some trigger notifications. How would you specify its lifecycle and the rules for each transition? | State diagram; transition table with allowed roles and triggered actions; invalid transitions handled; validated with business |
| SA-12 | Optional | Migration analysis | You're moving five years of data from a legacy system to a new platform over one weekend. Some legacy records are incomplete, and some codes have no equivalent in the new system. What would you specify to make the migration safe, and how would you confirm it succeeded? | Data profiling; mapping and code-conversion rules; cleansing decisions; dry runs; reconciliation counts and checks; rollback plan; business sign-off |
| SA-13 | Optional | Security requirements | A new application will store personal data and be used by internal staff, external partners, and administrators. What security requirements would you specify for each group? | **Perspective:** system security controls (BA-21, DA-10, and AI-02 cover personal data from other angles). Authentication per group (e.g. multi-factor); role-based authorisation and least privilege; data protection such as encryption and masking; audit logging |
| SA-14 | Optional | Performance analysis | Users complain that a frequently used screen has become slow, mostly at the start of the working day. How would you analyse the problem and specify a measurable improvement? | Gathers facts (when, who, volume); reviews monitoring and logs with tech; identifies the bottleneck; measurable target such as response time at peak load; tested before release |
| SA-15 | Mandatory | Issue analysis | A user reports that "the system lost my changes" and attaches a screenshot of an error message, but no one on the team can reproduce it. Walk me through how you would gather information and try to reproduce it, and what you would do if you still can't. | Collects steps, time, environment, and data; checks logs; tries different conditions; involves developers; if still not reproducible, adds logging, monitors, and keeps the user informed |
| SA-16 | Optional | Issue analysis | A user reports a bug: when they cancel a record, its related records stay open, but they expected them to close automatically. The story's acceptance criteria don't mention related records, and the user's manager says the old system always did this. How would you decide whether this is a defect or a change request, and how would you handle it either way? | Checks agreed requirements and AC; checks whether old behaviour was in scope; decides whether it's a requirements gap or a new request; defect process or change request accordingly; agrees with PO or business |
| SA-17 | Optional | Issue management | After a release, you find a bug that saves some records with the wrong status. It affects about one in ten users, and the only workaround is manual. How would you assess its severity and priority, and how would you communicate your findings to the team and stakeholders? | Distinguishes severity from priority; assesses business impact and data affected; documents the workaround; communicates facts clearly; plans the fix and data correction |
| SA-18 | Optional | Issue management | A fix for a user-reported issue went live yesterday, and today a different feature that shares the same component has stopped working. How would you handle the regression, and what would you change to protect system stability in future releases? | Assesses impact; rollback or hotfix; root cause; impact analysis of shared components; wider regression scope; improved test coverage |

## Data Analysis

| ID | Type | Competency | Question | Strong answer includes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| DA-01 | Mandatory | Elicitation of information | A business unit asks for "regular figures to track performance" with no further detail. Before any data work starts, what would you ask stakeholders, and why? | Decisions the figures support; audience; specific measures and definitions; level of detail; refresh frequency; data sources; success criteria |
| DA-02 | Mandatory | KPI definition | A stakeholder asks for a dashboard showing "engagement". How would you turn that into measurable KPIs that stakeholders agree on? | Links engagement to the business goal; breaks it into measurable indicators; definitions, calculation rules, data sources, and targets; agreement and documentation |
| DA-03 | Optional | Data lifecycle | A manager asks why the number of completed requests fell by 20% last quarter and wants an answer in two weeks. The data sits in two systems, and you don't yet have access to one of them. Walk me through how you would take this from the initial question to a finding the manager can act on. | Clarifies the question and hypotheses; requests access early; explores data; segments and compares trends; validates findings; clear, actionable conclusion within the deadline |
| DA-04 | Optional | BA artifacts documentation | A new reporting solution will draw on several data sources and serve three different audiences. Which artifacts would you produce to document its data requirements, and what would each contain? | Data dictionary; source-to-target mapping; business rules and calculations; report specifications per audience; data quality requirements; refresh and access rules |
| DA-05 | Optional | Data preparation | You receive a dataset where 15% of a key field is missing, dates come in different formats, and a few values are far outside the normal range. How would you prepare it for analysis, and how would you decide what to do about each problem? | Profiles the data; assesses impact of missing values; standardises formats; investigates outliers (error or real); decides to fix, exclude, or flag; documents assumptions |
| DA-06 | Optional | Data integration | You're combining data from three source systems that use different identifiers for the same records and update at different times of day. How would you match the records and make sure the combined data is consistent and accurate? | Matching rules or master identifier; mapping; handles timing differences; reconciliation checks; data quality rules; documented lineage |
| DA-07 | Mandatory | Data analysis | Two departments' monthly reports show different totals for the same measure, and each is sure theirs is correct. How would you investigate the discrepancy, resolve it, and make sure every team reports consistently from now on? | Compares definitions, sources, filters, and timing; reconciles step by step; agrees one definition and an owner; data dictionary; governance to keep it consistent |
| DA-08 | Optional | Data quality management | A live dashboard used for daily decisions has twice shown wrong figures after a source system failed overnight. What would you put in place so problems are caught before users see them? | Automated checks for completeness, freshness, and thresholds; alerts; data status shown on the dashboard; incident process; user communication |
| DA-09 | Optional | Data requirements | A source-system team plans to change the format and meaning of a field that several of your reports depend on. How would you assess the impact and manage the change? | Identifies dependent reports and users through lineage; assesses impact; agrees timing with the source team; updates mappings; tests; communicates |
| DA-10 | Optional | Data compliance | An analysis needs individual-level data that includes personal information. How would you make sure its use complies with the privacy regulations that apply? | **Perspective:** compliant use of data in analysis (BA-21, SA-13, and AI-02 cover personal data from other angles). Legal basis; data minimisation; anonymisation or pseudonymisation; access control; retention; involves the data protection function |
| DA-11 | Optional | SQL and data querying | Account details are in one table and transactions in another. You need a list of every account with its total transaction amount, including accounts with no transactions. How would you write the query, and what would change if you used JOIN instead of LEFT JOIN? | Joins the tables on the account ID; LEFT JOIN keeps accounts with no transactions, while JOIN drops them; GROUP BY account; SUM of the amount; shows 0 instead of an empty total |
| DA-12 | Optional | Reporting | A monthly report takes an analyst two days of manual spreadsheet work and has contained errors twice this year. How would you automate it so it's reliable and can scale to more data and users? | Understands the current process; automates extraction and transformation; validation checks; scheduling; version control; documentation; designed to scale |

## Project Manager

| ID | Type | Competency | Question | Strong answer includes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| PM-01 | Mandatory | Role understanding | You're hired as both BA and PM on a delivery project. How would you define the PM's responsibilities, how does the PM's focus differ from the BA's, and where might the two roles pull you in different directions? | PM owns delivery: plan, timeline, budget, risk, resources, reporting; BA owns the problem and requirements; tensions such as deadline versus requirement quality; how to manage them |
| PM-02 | Optional | Planning | You're both BA and PM on a new project with a fixed go-live date in six months. Requirements are known only at a high level, and two other teams must deliver parts of the solution. How would you organise your BA work and your PM work so that each feeds the other, rather than running them as two separate workloads? | One integrated plan with analysis milestones; analysis timed to delivery and other teams' deadlines; analysis outputs refine estimates and risks; delivery dates drive analysis priority |
| PM-03 | Optional | Resource and capacity management | Your team is fully booked for the next two sprints when the sponsor adds an urgent priority, and one of your two developers is about to go on leave. How would you rebalance the work, and what would you tell the affected stakeholders? | Reassesses priorities with the sponsor; capacity-based plan; explicit trade-offs on what moves out; transparent communication of impact |
| PM-04 | Optional | Dependency management | Your release depends on a component from another team, which has just told you it will be three weeks late. Your go-live date is fixed by contract. How would you manage the dependency and protect the date? | Impact on the critical path; options such as re-sequencing, stubs, or reduced scope; works with the other team; escalates early; informs the sponsor |
| PM-05 | Optional | Risk and issue management | Halfway through the project, a key supplier warns that their delivery might slip by two weeks but can't confirm it yet. How would you assess this risk, what would you do now while it's still uncertain, and how would you track it until it's resolved? | **Perspective:** risk tracking and the delivery plan (BA-22 covers impact on requirements and scope). Likelihood and impact; owner; mitigation and contingency plans; triggers to monitor; risk register; reported to leadership |
| PM-06 | Optional | Status reporting | Your project is three weeks behind plan, but the last two status reports showed it on track because the team expected to catch up. How would you report the real status to senior stakeholders now, and how would you report status from here on? | Reports the true status immediately; explains the cause and a recovery plan; takes ownership; objective status criteria such as defined RAG thresholds or burn-up charts |
| PM-07 | Optional | Project governance | The steering committee meets monthly and says it hears about problems too late, while the team says status requests already take hours each week. How would you give leadership the oversight it needs without overloading the team? | Lightweight regular reporting; dashboard drawn from delivery tools; escalation by exception between meetings; format agreed with the steering committee |

## Product Owner

| ID | Type | Competency | Question | Strong answer includes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| PO-01 | Mandatory | Role understanding | You're hired as both BA and PO on a product team. How would you define the PO's responsibilities, how does the PO's focus differ from the BA's, and where might the two roles pull you in different directions? | PO owns product value, vision, backlog priority, and acceptance; BA analyses needs and details requirements; tensions such as speed to market versus depth of analysis |
| PO-02 | Optional | Product vision | Leadership has set a one-line vision and a goal for the year for a new product, but the backlog is empty and the team starts in a month. How would you turn the vision into a backlog the team can start delivering against? | Breaks the vision into goals and outcomes; story mapping; epics; first increment or MVP; initial backlog refined with the team |
| PO-03 | Optional | Roadmapping | Leadership asks you to create the first 12-month roadmap for a new product. Which roadmapping techniques or formats would you use to build it, and why would you choose them for a product at this stage? | Named techniques such as now/next/later or outcome- and theme-based roadmaps; less precision for later horizons; explains why they suit an uncertain new product |
| PO-04 | Optional | Backlog prioritisation | The backlog has 40 requirements, but the MVP must launch in three months with capacity for about a third of them. The sponsor wants everything, a sales lead pushes features for one key client, and the tech lead flags several items as high effort. How would you decide what goes into the MVP based on business value, and how would you get that scope approved? | Value versus effort; named technique (e.g. MoSCoW, WSJF); weighs one client against the wider market; transparent rationale; sponsor approval |
| PO-05 | Optional | Stakeholder alignment | At the sprint review, a key stakeholder rejects a feature the team considers done, because it doesn't do something they had assumed it would. How would you resolve it now, and how would you prevent it from happening again? | Clarifies the assumption; checks AC and agreed scope; decides on a follow-up story or change; prevents recurrence through earlier involvement, demos, and clearer AC |
| PO-06 | Optional | Value delivery | Halfway through a feature, the team proposes a simpler version to meet the sprint deadline. It's quicker to build, but it may not deliver the outcome the business asked for. How would you decide whether to accept it? | Returns to the business outcome; assesses how much value the simpler version delivers; considers phased delivery; involves stakeholders; treats the deadline as one factor |

## Scrum Master

| ID | Type | Competency | Question | Strong answer includes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| SM-01 | Mandatory | Role understanding | You're hired as both BA and Scrum Master on a delivery team. How would you define the Scrum Master's responsibilities, how does that focus differ from the BA's, and where might the two roles pull you in different directions? | SM facilitates events, removes impediments, coaches the team, and protects its focus; BA focuses on requirement content; tension between staying neutral and holding a requirements view |
| SM-02 | Optional | Facilitation | The daily stand-up regularly runs 40 minutes, turns into problem-solving between two people, and half the team has stopped paying attention. What would you change? | Timebox; refocus on the sprint goal; move problem-solving to a follow-up; rotate facilitation; reinforce the stand-up's purpose |
| SM-03 | Optional | Retrospectives | For three sprints, retrospectives have raised the same complaints, and none of the agreed actions has been completed. What would you do differently? | Varies formats; few actionable items with owners and deadlines; tracks actions visibly; addresses root causes and psychological safety |
