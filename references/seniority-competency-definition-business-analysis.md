# Business Analysis — Seniority Competency Definition

Primary skill: Business Analysis. Levels in scope: **A2** Business Analyst · **A3** Senior Business Analyst (A1 and A4 are out of scope for candidate interviews and are not included).

## A2 vs A3 — Interview Calibration Summary

Derived from the General Requirements and Skill Requirements sections below; last generated 2026-10-20. Regenerate when those sections change.

| Area | A2 Business Analyst — what to listen for | A3 Senior Business Analyst — what to listen for |
|---|---|---|
| **Experience & scope** | Works at **feature** level within an established BA approach; ~1 year of E2E feature delivery (analysis, data model, documentation, approval, testing support); 6 months running elicitation; decomposes feature scope and estimates own tasks | Works at **component** level; 3+ years overall, incl. 1 year E2E component delivery; 2+ delivery projects; sets up the BA approach for the project; tracks delivery from business value to production use; improves the SDLC approach |
| **Elicitation** | Prepares, runs and captures sessions; selects techniques for the scope (hands-on with 2–3); validates information and obtains approvals | Leads discovery-type elicitation and adapts format to project/client goals; facilitates group events (workshops, focus groups, brainstorming); spots inconsistencies across requirements |
| **Stakeholders & client relations** | Builds credibility and trust; listens with empathy; summarises and rephrases; creates RACI/stakeholder artifacts; follows the communication plan | Recommends a stakeholder-management approach; designs the communication plan (geography, culture, formality, cadence); adapts style per audience; simplifies complex ideas; says no with valid reasoning; guides the client on solutions |
| **BA approach & planning** | Explains the approach to stakeholders; understands the problem, assumptions and constraints; plans own work (under supervision); follows defined roles and change process | Chooses the approach and level of formality; creates or adapts templates; defines BA roles and responsibilities; defines change management, verification/validation and approval processes; partners with PM on plans and timelines |
| **Requirements, change & traceability** | Creates LLD (use cases/user stories, GUI requirements, basic diagrams) without supervision; assesses impact of changes; uses business rules, functional decomposition, data modelling, backlog, acceptance criteria; keeps traceability up to date | Creates HLD, BPMN/UML diagrams, risk/impact analysis and risk log; contributes to DoR/DoD and issue workflow; proposes a change-management approach and communicates change impact; traces requirements backward and forward; designs how BA information is stored and reused |
| **Prioritisation, scope & estimation** | Uses MoSCoW/ranking; identifies decision-makers for approval; decomposes backlog and business requirements into FRs/NFRs; estimates own tasks (3-point, T-shirt, planning poker) | Builds stakeholder consensus on priorities (value, effort, dependencies, risk, user satisfaction); sets up and justifies the approval process; proposes MVP scope; supports phase planning; WBS/expert-judgment estimates, incl. high-level dev effort |
| **Domain & solution** | Experience in one domain segment; researches new domains; understands client needs and the proposed solution; aware of solution assessment | Knows one domain well enough to find gaps and bottlenecks; considers regulatory and risk factors; explains why a change is needed; contributes to solution definition; prioritises defects; proposes success criteria |
| **SDLC & IT knowledge** | Solid in one methodology; understands Agile vs non-Agile; adjusts BA processes to the SDLC; basics of client-server, databases, UX/UI | Solid in two or more methodologies; establishes a BA approach suited to the methodology; understands architecture styles (microservices, monolith, cloud), relational/non-relational databases, CI/CD; may support pre-sales |
| **Quality & risk** | Knows BA metrics; identifies and communicates risks to management | Measures BA work against KPIs; communicates risk impact of changes with mitigation options |
| **GenAI** | Uses approved GenAI tools with pre-built or lightly customised prompts to draft and inspect artifacts; cross-checks with multiple models; notes AI assumptions/limits; identifies simple use cases | Designs prompt strategies, reusable prompt packs and guardrails; defines evals/quality gates; integrates GenAI across BA workflows with measurable value; coaches the team and drives adoption |
| **Leadership & people** | Team management not applicable; ownership under supervision; recognises conflict and escalates when needed; adapts within a pre-structured framework | Steps in to lead teams; onboards/trains/manages at least 1 other BA; mentors and develops others; drives change and challenges the status quo; resolves conflict without escalation; facilitates team meetings |
| **English** | B2 | B2+ |

**Signals in interview answers**

- **Level of work:** A2 talks about a feature they delivered; A3 talks about a component or whole initiative and how its BA work was set up.
- **Follow vs define:** A2 follows or proposes tweaks to an existing approach, template or process; A3 explains why they chose or designed it.
- **Own vs others' work:** A2 plans and estimates their own tasks; A3 describes shaping other people's work — PM plans, other BAs, team ways of working.
- **Stakeholders:** A2 builds trust and escalates conflict; A3 adapts to each audience, pushes back with reasoning, and builds consensus.
- **GenAI:** A2 uses AI to draft and check; A3 builds reusable assets and governance for the team.

## General Requirements

### Professional Experience

**A2 Business Analyst**

- Experience working within the established BA approach and project information management ecosystem including the business analysis domain.
- Experience (6 months)  in preparation, conducting, and processing results of requirements elicitation activities on a Feature level(feature = working e2e a part of a system's component. e.g.: ' Create a new Customer record').
- Experience (1 year) in E2E BA processes/activities & artifacts delivery(analysis, attribute&data model setup, documentation, review & approval, implementation and testing maintenance)  on a Feature level.
- Experience (1 year) in BA feature scope decomposition(and justification about prioritization for work and decomposition)  and BA (own) tasks estimations.

**A3 Senior Business Analyst**

- Lead initiatives to streamline business processes for improved efficiency.
- Effective partnering with Project/Program management to input into project plans and timelines.
- Experience in setting up the BA approach aligned with the Project goals and delivery.
- Experience in E2E elicitation activities including the 'discovery' type of elicitation,  adapting format, dynamics, processes, activities, and artifacts according to the project and client goals.
- Overall BA experience of at least 3 years, including  1 year in E2E BA artifacts delivery (analysis, attribute&data model setup, documentation, review & approval, implementation, and testing maintenance)  on a Component level (component = working e2e a part of a system. e.g.: "Customer management module" or "Website catalog browse" or "Users ingest API".).
- Experience in completion of at least 2 delivery projects for 2+ years.
- Experience in identifying, orchestrating, and tracking E2E delivery activities, from defining business value and goals to implementing systems used by real production users.
- Experience in enhancing, and improving SDLC methodology strategic and/or tactical approaches on a project.

### Domain Expertise

**A2 Business Analyst**

- Experience in one domain's segment on process, organizational, functional, or logical levels.
- Experience in researching new domains.
- Solid experience in building domain-specific BA material through practical experience and successful project implementations.
- Demonstrated experience in eliciting and analyzing requirements specific to the target industry.

**A3 Senior Business Analyst**

- Experience in one domain on the level that allows to identify solution improvements, gaps, and bottlenecks.
- Has good knowledge in understanding the business domain structure, current practices, and activities.
- Has good knowledge of the domain's regulatory environment, and risk management considerations are integrated into business analysis processes.
- Experience in identifying major competitors and partners within the domain, supporting strategic analysis and collaboration.
- Experience in providing mentorship and guidance to junior and mid-level analysts to work efficiently with cross-functional teams (Not: about Domain ).
- Proficient in domain-specific tools (e.g., data modeling tools, user story mapping tools) and relevant industry-specific software. (e.g. experience with project management software).

### Client Relations

**A2 Business Analyst**

- Able to build credibility and trust with stakeholders.
- Able to effectively communicate how the proposed solution will achieve business objectives.
- Built positive relationships with customers by actively listening to their concerns and demonstrating empathy.
- Ability to summarize and rephrase.

**A3 Senior Business Analyst**

- Able to manage complex interactions and connections with customers to drive satisfaction and loyalty.
- Adapts behavior, communication style, and interactions with stakeholders based on their needs.
- Able to simplify and adapt complex ideas to the audience level.
- Develop a communication plan considering geography, culture, the formality of the organization, and the cadence of stakeholders.
- Ability to not be afraid to say no to a client with valid reasoning.
- Experience in guiding the customer about solutions.
- May present as a functional expert in a product.

### Team Management

**A2 Business Analyst**

Not applicable for this level

**A3 Senior Business Analyst**

- Able to step in and lead teams with confidence and ensure effective communication and collaboration among team members.
- Experience in onboarding/training/managing at least 1 other BA on the project.

### AI-Based Solutions

**A2 Business Analyst**

Surface simple, low-risk use cases and prototype prompt variations; provide feedback on output quality, propose solutions.

**A3 Senior Business Analyst**

Design task-specific prompt strategies/automations, pilot and scale successful patterns, and maintain evaluation checklists and metrics. Apply these to your domain/ specialisation to identify clear impacts on business processes and drive the adoption of GEN AI.

### Day-to-Day AI Enablement

**A2 Business Analyst**

Use approved GenAI tools with pre-built or lightly customized prompts to 1. Draft 2. Inspect BA artifacts. Use multiple models to verify accuracy/relevance and follow security/ethics.

**A3 Senior Business Analyst**

Build reusable prompt packs/playbooks, standardized guardrails, and coach teams on effective, ethical use and validation. Track reusable prompts for adoption within the team.

### Project Delivery / SDLC

**A2 Business Analyst**

Apply GenAI within defined workflows for elicitation, documentation, agile support, and basic data analysis; note AI assumptions/limits and escalate risks.

**A3 Senior Business Analyst**

Integrate GenAI across BA workflows (backlog, ACs, traceability) with quality gates, governance, and measurable value realization across streams. Ability to define clear guardrails/ evals for agents/ prompts other Gen AI artefacts.

#### Elicitation Techniques

Elicitation Techniques are structured methods used to gather accurate, complete, and actionable information from stakeholders, users, and subject matter experts. They support the discovery and clarification of needs, constraints, assumptions, and priorities before design or implementation decisions are made. Common elicitation approaches include interviews, workshops, surveys, observation, document analysis, and brainstorming, each suited to different contexts and stakeholder groups. Effective elicitation requires active listening, clear questioning, neutrality, and the ability to detect ambiguity or hidden expectations. In engineering practices, these techniques help align solutions with business objectives, reduce rework, and improve shared understanding across teams. They are applied throughout the lifecycle, from initial discovery through refinement and change management, to ensure requirements remain relevant and traceable.

Key features

 - Uses multiple methods to collect information based on stakeholder availability, complexity, and risk.

 - Focuses on uncovering explicit requirements as well as implicit needs, constraints, and assumptions.

 - Relies on active listening and purposeful questioning to improve clarity and completeness.

 - Supports collaboration by creating a common understanding among diverse stakeholders.

 - Helps identify conflicts, gaps, and inconsistencies early in the development lifecycle.

 - Produces inputs for requirements definition, solution design, validation, and prioritization.

 - Can be adapted for different levels of detail, from high-level discovery to detailed specification.

 - Benefits from facilitation skills to manage group dynamics and maintain productive discussion.

 - Improves decision quality by grounding choices in verified stakeholder information.

- **A2 Business Analyst:** Intermediate (Optional)
  - Has hands-on experience with 2-3 of the elicitation techniques and has theoretical knowledge in 2-3 more.
  - Able to select the elicitation technique - for example, Document Analysis, Interface Analysis, Observation, Interview, Prototyping.
- **A3 Senior Business Analyst:** Advanced (Optional)
  - Has an understanding of the differences between requirements gathering and elicitation.
  - Has proven facilitation and interviewing skills.
  - Has hands-on experience of managing group elicitation events by using different group techniques - for example, Brainstorming, Mind-mapping, Delphi Technique, Requirements workshop, Focus Groups, Survey/Questionnaire.

#### Requirements and Change management

Requirements and Change Management is the discipline and set of coordinated practices for capturing, controlling, assessing, and communicating modifications to requirements throughout a project or product lifecycle, ensuring that proposed changes are analyzed for impact, authorized through formal workflows, traced to related artifacts, and implemented in a controlled, auditable way that maintains stakeholder alignment and minimizes risk. Main features: 

 - Requirements capture, documentation and baselining

 - Change request logging, categorization and triage

 - Impact analysis across requirements, design, code and tests

 - Approval workflows, change control boards and authorization

 - Traceability and bi-directional links between artifacts

 - Versioning, configuration management and release coordination

 - Stakeholder communication, notifications and decision records

 - Prioritization, negotiation and scope/risk assessment

 - Integration with ALM, issue-tracking and CI/CD tools

 - Auditable history, metrics and reporting for compliance and insight

- **A2 Business Analyst:** Novice (Required)
  - Assesses the impact of changes to requirements.
  - Requirements management techniques:
  - Business rules analysis
  - Functional decomposition
  - Data modeling
  - Backlog management
  - Item tracking
  - Acceptance and evaluation criteria
- **A3 Senior Business Analyst:** Intermediate (Core)
  - Proposes a change management approach based on chosen project methodology.
  - Analyses and communicates the impact of requirement changes to the stakeholders.
  - Requirements management techniques:
  - Data Flow Diagrams
  - Business cases
  - Reviews

#### Efforts estimation

Efforts estimation is the practice of forecasting the time, labor, and other resources required to complete a defined unit of work. In requirements lifecycle management, it supports planning by translating requirements, assumptions, and constraints into actionable delivery estimates. The skill combines domain knowledge, historical data, decomposition of work, and judgment to produce estimates that are realistic enough for prioritization, budgeting, and scheduling. Effective estimation accounts for scope, complexity, dependencies, uncertainty, risk, and team capacity. It is typically refined as requirements mature and more information becomes available. Strong effort estimation improves decision making, helps align stakeholder expectations, and provides a baseline for tracking delivery performance.

Key features

 - Uses requirements analysis to identify the scope and complexity of the work to be estimated.

 - Applies estimation techniques such as expert judgment, analogy, parametric models, and work breakdown structures.

 - Incorporates uncertainty by expressing ranges, confidence levels, or assumptions rather than fixed values only.

 - Relies on historical delivery data and team performance trends to improve accuracy.

 - Accounts for dependencies, constraints, rework, and non-functional requirements that affect effort.

 - Supports iterative refinement as requirements are clarified, changed, or decomposed.

 - Provides input for planning, prioritization, budgeting, staffing, and release forecasting.

 - Requires clear documentation of assumptions, exclusions, and estimation basis for stakeholder alignment.

- **A2 Business Analyst:** Intermediate (Required)
  - Estimates effort required for own BA tasks.
  - Familiar with at least two of the following: 3 point, T-shirt size, Planning poker.
  - Performs estimation on task level (story, use case etc.).
- **A3 Senior Business Analyst:** Advanced (Required)
  - Estimates effort required for BA tasks.
  - Estimates development efforts at a high level (optional as this is relevant for BA being long at one project).
  - Familiar with WBS-based effort estimate, expert judgment, use case points.

#### Leadership

Leadership is a multifaceted concept that encompasses a set of competencies enabling one to lead, influence, and guide individuals, teams, or entire organizations.

Leadership involves the ability to:

 - Influence others: Persuading and motivating people to work toward a common goal or vision.

 - Guide teams and organizations: Steering groups in diverse contexts - be it in business, politics, the military, or non-profit sectors - toward success.

 - Inspire confidence and trust: Cultivating an environment where team members feel valued and committed to shared objectives.

 - Adapt to change: Demonstrating flexibility and resilience in dynamic and often complex environments.

The concept of leadership is not restricted to any single definition or framework; rather, it embodies a range of theories and practices that can vary according to context, culture, and individual style. Whether viewed as an innate ability or a set of learned behaviors, leadership plays a crucial role in shaping strategies, empowering teams, and driving organizational success.

- **A2 Business Analyst:** Not applicable

  - Emerges as a leader based on feedback from peers and managers.
  - Directs the group toward a common goal and takes responsibility for team/group outcomes.
  - Efficiently organizes teamwork and engages employees in collaborative initiatives.
